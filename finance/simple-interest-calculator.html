How it works

Simple interest = Principal × Rate × Time, with the rate as a decimal and time in years.

Example: $5,000 at 4% for 3 years earns 5,000 × 0.04 × 3 = $600 of interest.

Frequently asked questions

When is simple interest used?

Some short-term loans, certain bonds and basic savings examples use simple interest. Most savings accounts compound.

How is it different from compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal; compound interest also earns on previously earned interest.
